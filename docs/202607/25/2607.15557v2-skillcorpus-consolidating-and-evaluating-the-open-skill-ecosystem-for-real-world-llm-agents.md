## 摘要
Agent技能，即封装了可复用程序性知识以供LLM Agent使用的$\texttt{SKILL.md}$文件，是一种流行的扩展Agent能力机制。公共仓库现以庞大且不断增长的数量托管这些技能，但这些工件碎片化、冗余、质量参差不齐，且其实际价值尚不明确。一个核心问题仍然存在，即如何将这个开源的$\texttt{SKILL.md}$生态系统整合为单一可用的语料库，以及它对真实世界Agent任务益处的界限何在。我们提出$\textbf{SkillCorpus}$，一个大规模聚合、整理、匹配和评估开放技能生态系统的框架。它通过多阶段流水线将约82.1万个爬取技能筛选为96,401个技能，按16类分类法和三个质量维度（实用性、鲁棒性、安全性）组织，并搭配一个经过微调的检索-选择栈，用于匹配任务相关技能。我们在三个基准测试（SkillsBench、GDPVal、QwenClawBench）、两个测试框架和两个开放主干模型上进行了端到端评估，并进行了前沿鲁棒性检查。整合SkillCorpus在所有三个基准测试中均带来一致的性能提升，在SkillsBench上提升最大（+7.5个百分点）。运营分析将增益归因于覆盖边界和测试框架边界。据我们所知，SkillCorpus首次端到端地阐述了经过整理、检索服务的社区语料库何时能改善真实Agent任务，以及何时不能。数据集、模型和代码将在录用后发布。

## Abstract
Agent skills, $\texttt{SKILL.md}$ files that package reusable procedural knowledge for an LLM agent, are a popular mechanism for extending agent capabilities. Public repositories now host them in large and growing numbers, yet these artifacts are fragmented, redundant, and uneven in quality, and their value in practice is unclear. A core question remains open, namely how to consolidate this open-source $\texttt{SKILL.md}$ ecosystem into a single usable corpus, and what bounds its benefit on real-world agent tasks. We present $\textbf{SkillCorpus}$, a framework that aggregates, curates, matches, and evaluates the open skill ecosystem at scale. It filters ~821,000 crawled skills through a multi-stage pipeline into 96,401 skills organised by a 16-class taxonomy and three quality facets (utility, robustness, safety), and pairs them with a fine-tuned retrieval-and-selection stack that matches task-relevant skills. We evaluate end-to-end across three benchmarks (SkillsBench, GDPVal, QwenClawBench), two harnesses, and two open backbones with a frontier robustness check. Integrating SkillCorpus yields consistent gains across all three benchmarks, largest on SkillsBench (+7.5 pp). An operational analysis traces the gains to a coverage boundary and a harness boundary. SkillCorpus is, to our knowledge, the first end-to-end account of when a curated, retrieval-served community corpus improves real agent tasks, and where it does not. The dataset, models, and code will be released upon acceptance.
